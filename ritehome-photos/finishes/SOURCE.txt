Why the gloss file was cropped
------------------------------
The upload was a whole door, and a door carries what a sample does not: a
drawer seam at y=156 and the grey studio floor at all four edges. Both
landed inside the swatch frame and read as artefacts next to three faces
that are pure material. Cut to the door face itself, x 6..434, y 166..780,
JPEG q94. The crop was done in a headless Chromium canvas - ffmpeg on this
machine will not decode a progressive JPEG, and there is no ImageMagick or
PIL. Cropping the file rather than zooming past the seam in CSS keeps the
face sharp and lets one plain `cover` rule hold at every width.

Also fixed: .sw-face::before was background-size:auto 128%
---------------------------------------------------------
That covered the four tall strips of the desktop wall but NOT the 2x2 of
squat tiles the wall becomes under 820px - the photo sat short of the tile
and the drawn .f-wood / .f-gloss fallback showed down both sides. It is
`cover` now, which holds at any aspect; --face-pos still chooses what is in
frame, since cover always leaves slack on one axis. Verified at 1440, 780
and 400 px wide.

Processing
----------
Source renders are 1372x1868 staged sheet-on-grey shots with an "A4"
badge. Cropped to the sheet interior (x 330, y 420, 790 x 1230), resized
to 760x1183, JPEG q88, with PowerShell System.Drawing (no ImageMagick on
this machine). Script: scratchpad/crop_all.ps1 in the session that made
these. The page oversizes each face (background-size:auto 128%) and gives
each a --face-pos, so nothing tiles obviously.

Replacing any of these with Ritehome's own photographs
------------------------------------------------------
Shoot the chip flat, filling the frame, light from one side. Then point
the matching rule in index.html at the new file (currently, per the
2026-09-14 update above):

    .swatch.sw-solid{--face:url("ritehome-photos/finishes/solid-green-kitchen.jpg");...}
    .swatch.sw-matte{--face:url("ritehome-photos/finishes/matte-white.jpg");...}
    .swatch.sw-wood {--face:url("ritehome-photos/finishes/wood-walnut-dark.jpg"); ...}
    .swatch.sw-gloss{--face:url("ritehome-photos/finishes/gloss-white.jpg");...}

All four are in-situ crops rather than flat sample chips. A chip shot
flat, filling the frame, lit from one side would beat every one of them -
that remains the upgrade path.

Nothing else has to change - the finish overlay, the hover rake and the
board-core edge all sit above the face and work over a photograph as they
do over the drawn fallback (.f-solid / .f-matte / .f-wood / .f-gloss).
