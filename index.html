15 — Material

Four faces.

Solid colours, a matte surface, woodgrain and high gloss — the four faces we run. Move across a sample; the light rakes low, the same way a finish is checked on the bench.

The warm band along the bottom of each sample is the board core — the colour you only meet at a cut, and the colour the edgebanding is there to close. Counters run quartz, natural granite or synthetic, priced by the slab your run actually consumes.

16 — Module

Modular does not
mean less custom.

It means the carcass is built to your dimensions in the shop, on a bench, under controlled conditions — then carried in finished and set. Backs are the same 18 mm board as the carcass, not a 3 mm skin. It costs more, and it is the reason a cabinet still sits square after it has been loaded.

18 — Brief

Renovation anxiety is rarely about taste. It is about not knowing.

Not knowing what the number covers. Not knowing whether the finish on the last cabinet will match the first. Not knowing what changed between the day you agreed and the day it arrived. We built the company around removing that, which is why the drawing and the itemised quantities come before the price, and why nothing is fabricated on your floor.

19 — Sequence

Four stages, each with a name attached.

A job moves through four tracked stages. At any point you can ask which stage yours is in and who is holding it, and get an answer rather than an estimate.

01

Drafting

Drafter

Site measured, elevations drawn, revisions logged. Ends when you approve the sheet.

02

Procurement

Procurement officer

Boards, edging, hardware and accessories bought against the approved bill of quantities.

03

Fabrication

Fabricator

Cut, edged and assembled in our own shop by our own salaried crew. Not subcontracted.

04

Installation

Installer

Delivered finished, levelled and set. Your floor sees assembly, not a workshop.

21 — Enquiry

Start with the measurement.

Tell us the room and where you are. A site visit is arranged from there, and the drawing follows the visit.

Three measurements worth having ready before the first call: the full length of each wall the run meets, the ceiling height, and the position of any window, door or service point along that wall.
