Renovation anxiety is rarely about taste. It is about not knowing.

Not knowing what the number covers. Not knowing whether the finish on the last cabinet will match the first. Not knowing what changed between the day you agreed and the day it arrived. We built the company around removing that, which is why the drawing and the itemised quantities come before the price, and why nothing is fabricated on your floor.

01 — Drafting

The drawing is the product.

Before material is discussed, your space is measured and drawn as a dimensioned elevation. Every carcass, every door swing, every filler against an out-of-square wall is resolved on the sheet, where a change costs a revision number rather than a panel.

Each drawing carries a revision counter and a status. When it moves, you see the new number. Nothing goes to procurement until the sheet you approved is the sheet being cut from.

03 — Module

Modular does not mean less custom.

It means the carcass is built to your dimensions in the shop, on a bench, under controlled conditions — then carried in finished and set. The dust, the offcuts and the margin for error stay with us.

Backs are the same 18 mm board as the carcass, not a 3 mm skin. It costs more and it is the reason a cabinet still sits square after it has been loaded.

08 — Enquiry

Start with the measurement.

Tell us the room and where you are. A site visit is arranged from there, and the drawing follows the visit.

Three measurements worth having ready before the first call: the full length of each wall the run meets, the ceiling height, and the position of any window, door or service point along that wall.
